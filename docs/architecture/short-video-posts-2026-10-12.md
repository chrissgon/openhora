## 1. What the person wants

1. Ask for a script for a post idea, in each platform's own format: Instagram Reels, TikTok, and a carousel or static post where that fits better.
2. Record the video themselves.
3. Hand the recording over. The system edits it: it cuts silences, burns in captions, and adds animations that illustrate what is said (kinetic text, icons, simple motion graphics tied to the transcript).
4. Get back the edited video with the post text (caption, hashtags), and be asked for approval.
5. On approval, the publication is scheduled on the platform or platforms.

## 3. Platform facts (official sources, read 2026-10-12)

Each fact names its source (section 14). "Not stated" means the page read did not say. "Unverified" means it comes from a secondary source or could not be read.

### 3.1 Instagram (Instagram Platform, Content Publishing)

- **Account.** A professional account, Business or Creator [IG-overview].
- **Two logins** [IG-overview]:
  - Instagram Login (`graph.instagram.com`), no Facebook Page needed.
  - Facebook Login for Business (`graph.facebook.com`), where the account "must be connected to a Facebook Page".
- **Access.** Standard Access is enough for an app that serves only accounts owned or managed by people with a role on the app. Advanced Access "requires App Review and Business Verification" and is needed only for other people's accounts [IG-overview].
- **Tokens.**
  - Long-lived Page access tokens "do not have an expiration date" [FB-longlived].
  - Data access is separate from authentication: it has a 90-day expiration period, based on when the user was last active, while the user token lasts about 60 days after last use [FB-authdata]. The page's list of non-expiring permissions does not name `instagram_basic` or `instagram_content_publish` [FB-authdata].
  - **Implication:** a Page token can still stop working at the slot. The publisher's `--check` reports both dates (P8, P9).
- **App mode.** "Apps in Development mode can only request permissions from role users" [FB-appmodes]. Whether such an app can publish publicly to the owner's own account is **not stated officially (unverified)**. P9's done-when checks that a post is visible to a logged-out viewer.
- **Going Live.** The basic-settings page says contact email, Terms of Service URL, app icon and category are each required to switch an app to Live mode [FB-basic]. It does not state the privacy policy URL as required.
- **Permissions** [IG-publishing]:
  - Instagram Login: `instagram_business_basic`, `instagram_business_content_publish`.
  - Facebook Login: `instagram_basic`, `instagram_content_publish`, `pages_read_engagement`.
- **Reels.** A container with `media_type=REELS` and a `video_url`, then `media_publish`. The media "must be hosted on a publicly accessible server at the time of the attempt" [IG-publishing].
  - **Resumable upload of a local file** (`upload_type=resumable`, host `rupload.facebook.com`) is "Only for apps that have implemented Facebook Login for Business" [IG-publishing].
  - **Implication:** without a Facebook Page, the rendered video has to be put at a public URL. That needs hosting, a hosting credential, and leaves a public copy of an unpublished video.
- **Container status:** `EXPIRED`, `ERROR`, `FINISHED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `PUBLISHED`; poll "once per minute, for no more than 5 minutes" [IG-publishing]. Containers expire after 24 hours [IG-media]. **Implication:** the container is created at the slot time, inside the scheduled job, never at approval.
- **Limits:**
  - "100 API-published posts within a 24-hour moving period". The carousel section of the same page says 50; the page does not explain the difference [IG-publishing].
  - Live usage: `GET /<IG_ID>/content_publishing_limit`. The plan reads the limit live from it and does not choose between 100 and 50.
- **Scheduling.** No scheduling parameter on the pages read. The page tells apps that "schedule posts" to enforce the limit themselves [IG-publishing]. A third-party tutorial claims `scheduled_publish_time`: **unverified**. Scheduling goes through `scheduler:job`.
- **Reels video spec** [IG-media]:
  - Container: MOV or MP4, no edit lists, moov atom at the front.
  - Video: H.264 or HEVC, progressive, closed GOP, 4:2:0.
  - Audio: AAC, at most 48 kHz.
  - 23 to 60 fps; width at most 1920; 9:16 recommended.
  - At most 25 Mbps video; 3 seconds to 15 minutes; at most 300 MB.
- **Caption** [IG-media]: at most 2,200 characters, 30 hashtags, 20 @ tags. `cover_url` or `thumb_offset` for the cover. `share_to_feed`.
- **Images** [IG-publishing]: JPEG only. No shopping tags, no filters. The carousel item count was **not checked**.

### 3.2 TikTok (Content Posting API)

- **Direct Post** needs the scope `video.publish`. Content from unaudited clients is "restricted to private viewing mode" [TT-direct-start].
  - Unaudited clients: "up to 5 users to post in a 24 hour window"; accounts must be private; `SELF_ONLY` only [TT-guidelines].
  - Posting cap: "typically around 15 posts per day per creator account" [TT-guidelines].
- **What every client must do** [TT-guidelines]:
  - Show a preview.
  - The person "must manually select the privacy status from a dropdown and there should be no default value".
  - Send content "only ... after the user has expressly consent[ed] to the upload".
  - Show the creator's nickname from fresh creator info.
  - Let the person edit preset text.
  - Add no brand name, logo or watermark of its own.
  - **Inference, not a stated rule:** fully unattended posting at a later time is at odds with these requirements.
- **Upload to inbox** (draft) needs the scope `video.upload` (the app "must be approved" for it). The video lands in the inbox, and the person "must click on inbox notifications to continue the editing flow in TikTok and complete the post" [TT-upload]. A separate audit for this flow is **not stated** on that page. The Upload Video reference states: "There may be at most 5 pending shares within any 24-hour period", with the error code `spam_risk_too_many_pending_share` [TT-upload-ref].
- **Direct Post reference** [TT-direct-ref]:
  - `post_info.privacy_level` is required and must match the creator info options.
  - `title` is at most 2,200 UTF-16 runes.
  - **No scheduling parameter.**
  - The init endpoint allows 6 requests per minute per token.
  - `upload_url` is valid for one hour.
  - Errors include `spam_risk_too_many_posts` and `unaudited_client_can_only_post_to_private_accounts`.
- **Media** [TT-media]:
  - MP4 recommended (WebM, MOV); H.264 recommended (H.265, VP8, VP9).
  - 23 to 60 fps; each side 360 to 4096 px; at most 4 GB; up to 10 minutes through the API.
  - FILE_UPLOAD in chunks of 5 to 64 MB (last up to 128 MB), 1 to 1,000 chunks, sent in sequence. PULL_FROM_URL needs a verified domain.

### 3.3 Carousel and static posts

The script step (step 1) can write slide texts for a carousel. Publishing one needs a sequence of images (JPEG only on Instagram [IG-publishing]). No skill makes those images today: `design-execute` needs a design tool and an image generator (`packs/design.txt` comment). Carousel and static publishing are left out of the first packages (decision D13).

**What fits a deterministic renderer driven by JSON:** ffmpeg (silencedetect, trim/concat, scale, crop, overlay) plus the HTML overlay engine (Chrome for Testing) for captions and animations, the renderer and the transcription from whisper.cpp each run in a provider-owned container (D22, D20). libass and `drawtext` stay as the fallback for captions only. Every piece is a separate process (the transcriber and the renderer each a separate container) with a permissive or copyleft licence that the workbench calls and does not ship; the workbench ships the Dockerfiles of the transcriber and the renderer, not a binary or an image.

- Assumption, not legal advice, for the maintainer to confirm: calling a GPL-3.0 `ffmpeg` binary as a separate process puts no licence condition on the MIT workbench, which does not distribute it.
- The workbench builds only what links the pieces: the edit-list schema, its validator, the base-list builder, and the compiler from the resolved list to an ASS file and an ffmpeg command.

## 5. Three paths, and the decision

Each path is described in full so that a reader can see why two of them lost. "In a run" means inside the eval container, with reading tools, no credential and no network beyond the model provider (`contracts/runtime.md`, "The contained run"; `AGENTS.md:145`). "Code" means deterministic code on the person's machine, outside any run: a provider, an operation, a handler.

All three paths share three things:
- Step 1 (`mkt-social-copy` writing a script and a caption).
- Step 2 (the person records).
- Step 5 (publisher providers through `scheduler:job`).

They differ in step 3, and in what step 4 shows.

### 5.5 Decision: path 1, with conditions C1 to C5 (round 1 of review)

Path 1 is decided. It is the only path that meets the whole of step 3 without moving a hard limit out of code (principle 11 first) or sending the raw recording off the machine. It changes nothing that the measurement fingerprint covers (principle 12). It adopts every media tool and builds only what links them (principle 14). Paths 2 and 3a are rejected for the reasons in 5.2 and 5.3. Path 3b is not kept as a fallback: M1 is path 1's own deterministic base. Since D21, a hosted editing service is excluded only as the default, not as an implementation: it can be added later behind `renderer:video` and chosen by the person per project.

The conditions:

- **C1, an edit list of indices.** The model writes only transcript word and segment indices, preset names from closed lists, typed parameter values (a `text`, a `choice` value, an `icon` name, a `color` token, an asset slot description) and short overlay labels. Code derives every time and every caption from `transcript.json`; a clip's in and out times are set by the person on the card, never by the model and never in the script. The validator refuses any time or caption text the model wrote. Labels and texts stay model text: code escapes them for every renderer and bounds their length. Since amendment A4, **silence (`silencedetect`) is the primary signal for cuts and the words only protect speech**: a cut may fall inside a stretched word span when there is silence inside it, no kept range is shorter than 0.4 s, each cut has a 10 to 20 ms audio fade, the margins are tuned (C2), and a detected retake (a run of at least 4 normalised words recurring within about 20 s, or a sentence cut mid-way and restarted) is proposed by code and removed only when the person confirms it. Code still derives every cut and every time; auto-editor is not adopted.
- **C2, a quality check before the presets are frozen.** The base edit list, with the animations of the script's beats, is rendered with the HTML engine on two or three of the person's own recordings. The person judges captions and animations together, and the render time is measured. #### P5c-0. The renderer slice that lets the script carry the new fields (before or with P3b)
P3b alone would break the renderer: `_align.read_beats` refuses unknown beat lines and undeclared parameters, the beats schema in `edl.schema.json` is closed, and `check_script.py` has `RESERVED = {beat, line, on-screen, preset}`, so a beat that carried `role` or `emphasis` would be read as a parameter of its preset and refused.
- **Beat keys:** `role` and `emphasis` join `line`, `on-screen` and `preset` as reserved beat keys in `check_script.py`; `_align.read_beats` accepts them; the beats schema gains them as optional closed enums (`role` from the vocabulary's list, `emphasis` `none|punch`). Recommended: the beats schema stays version 1, since the fields are optional and a beats file without them stays valid; the package states it.
- **Script-level fields get a header** at the top of the script block, before the first beat: one `key: value` line each, keys from a closed list, each at most once, in any order: `hook_type`, `title`, `cta_kind`, `send_to`, `loop`, `pattern`. `hook_type` and `title` are required for a `reel` or `video`; `cta_kind` is required when a `cta` beat exists; the others are optional. `check_script.py` reads and checks the header. `align`, which already runs the post's own checker, copies `title` and `loop` into a new optional top-level `script` object of `beats.json` (closed keys, bounded text); the rest stays in the post file.
- **The vocabulary file goes to version 2.** `shared/references/video-presets.json` gains a `script` section (A5's closed lists, thresholds and language data, and later P5d's preset names). Every reader of version 1 must not read a version 2 file silently, so it is a version change, not an addition. `_vocab.py` asserts `vocab["version"] == 1`; P5c-0 changes that assert to accept 2 and tests both.
- **Order of the pipeline:** `base` (cuts and captions from the transcript; it never reads the script) then `align` (reads the post and the transcript, matches the beats, **computes the caption style per range** and writes it into `beats.json`; P5c) then `render`. The style is not an edit-list field and `base` is not involved.
- **Tests (offline):** a beat with `role` and `emphasis` is read; an unknown value, an unknown beat key and an undeclared parameter are still refused; a header with an unknown or repeated key is refused; `beats.json` validates with and without the new fields; `_vocab.py` accepts version 2 and refuses 3; the copy of the checker that `align` runs isolated in the container reads the header.
- **Class:** provider and a script of `mkt-social-copy` (P3b carries that change). **Depends on:** nothing. **Done when:** a hand-written script with roles, emphasis and a header passes `align` and the roles appear in `beats.json`.

#### P3b. Script vocabulary v2 (a Y change of `mkt-social-copy`)
Roles, not times: the script gains meaning that code turns into timing. The research found the structural advice mostly vendor or blog opinion with no checkable sample, so these are **defaults**, tuned on real scripts, never rules of nature. Platform statements are the firmest ground: Instagram names watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as top Reels signals (second-hand [IG-signals]); TikTok's ads guidance says to hook within 6 s, state the core message within 3 s, use captions and end with one call to action [TT-ads].
- **New fields, all choices or short text, none a time (C1 holds):**
  - beat `role` (defined below); beat `emphasis`: `none` or `punch` (a punch-in on that beat, drawn by the framing layer of P5d; **at most 1 per 3 beats and at least 25 words, 10 s at the speech rate below, between two punches**, refused otherwise). Emphasis **words** in captions are not a script field: code picks them (P5c);
  - header: `hook_type` (`contrarian`, `mistake`, `result`, `curiosity`, `number`, `question`, `story`); `title` (3 to 5 words; the text of the first frame, opaque at frame 0 with no fade, when the first beat names no preset; the candidate cover text); `cta_kind` (`save`, `send`, `comment`, `follow-up-video`) with `send_to` (text, at most 40 characters) for `send`; `loop` (yes or no: the last line joins the first, no goodbye words, no end card); `pattern`, optional (`list`, `myth`, `story`, `problem-solution`, `before-after`, `steps`).
- **The roles**, each with a fictional example line (the function matters, not the wording):

  | Role | What the beat does | Example line |
  |---|---|---|
  | `hook` | the first beat: the promise or the claim | "Most people rehearse the wrong interview answer." |
  | `setup` | the context the point needs | "I have coached engineers through hiring loops for years, and one habit sinks them." |
  | `point` | one idea that keeps the hook's promise | "First, answer in the order the interviewer asked." |
  | `proof` | an example, a number or a quoted verse that backs a point | "In one mock round, switching the order made the answer half as long." |
  | `turn` | the reversal or the twist | "But the real problem is not the order." |
  | `payoff` | the answer the hook opened; sits in the last third | "Lead with the decision, then give the reason, and the answer stays short." |
  | `cta` | the one ask, the last beat | "Send this to someone preparing for an interview this week." |
- **One speech rate everywhere: 2.5 words per second** (150 words a minute; the research's inference), a constant in the vocabulary file. The checker runs before any recording, so it cannot use a measured rate; rates measured on real recordings may recalibrate the constant later, by a version change of the vocabulary file. 6 s is 15 words, 8 s is 20 words, 10 s is 25 words.
- **The eleven rules.** Each is split into what `check_script.py` enforces, with a **closed predicate** (a digit token; a word of a per-language list in the vocabulary file: openers, negations, number words, stop words; a quoted span; a count), and a **quality criterion** for the person or the reference model, which no script judges. Language data starts with pt-BR.

  | # | The checker enforces | Quality criterion |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 Open with the promise | beat 1 has role `hook`; its line has at most 18 words and no opener phrase from the list; by `hook_type`: `number` has a digit token in its first sentence, `question` ends with `?`, `mistake` and `contrarian` contain a negation word | the line makes a claim a viewer wants resolved; a `result` or `curiosity` hook withholds the method |
  | 2 The title mirrors the key phrase | `title` has 3 to 5 words, each a word of beat 1's line; beat 1's on-screen text, if any, has 2 to 5 words of that line | the title is the phrase a viewer would remember |
  | 3 Open a loop, close it late | a `payoff` beat exists, comes after more than half of the beats, and its line holds a content word of `title` (a word not on the stop-word list) | the payoff answers the hook, not merely repeats it |
  | 4 One idea per beat | a line over 30 words is refused; a line under 8 words warns, except for a `turn` | one idea per beat |
  | 5 A visual event at least every 6 s | a warning when a run of consecutive beats without a preset or `emphasis: punch` holds more than 15 words (the renderer adds punch-ins and style changes of its own, so only script-declared events count, and it warns) | events fall where the idea changes |
  | 6 Vary presets | no single preset on more than half of the beats; `list` has 2 to 4 items; a `number` figure is a digit token of the material (`--material`) | each preset serves its idea |
  | 7 Match the pattern to the roles | when `pattern` is declared, the roles of its row in the vocabulary file are present (`list`: a hook, a `payoff` or `cta`, and as many `point` beats as the hook's digit token promises; `myth`: a hook, a `turn`, a `proof`; `before-after`: two `compare` beats, accepted once P5d adds that preset) | the `turn` really states the truth |
  | 8 End on one CTA | exactly one `cta` beat, last, at most 20 words; `cta_kind` present; `send_to` present and at most 40 characters for `send` | the line before it is the payoff, not a recap; the ask is specific |
  | 9 The register leaves room | the skill reads the project's `docs/brand/voice.md` and passes `--register calm|neutral` (neutral, labelled `Assumption`, when the file is absent or silent). With `calm`: at most one preset per 20 words, `emphasis: none` on `turn` and `payoff`, and a quoted span uses a `quote` preset with its reference as author | the script fits the voice file |
  | 10 No invented facts | every digit token of a spoken line is a digit token of the material | every name and every quoted verse exists in the material, or is left out and raised as an `OPEN-<n>` question by the skill's existing grounding step. **`## Stop rules` is unchanged; a verse does not enter Stop rule 4.** |
  | 11 Length | a **warning, never a refusal**, when the spoken words fall outside 150 to 225 (the default target of 60 to 90 s); 120 s (300 words) is allowed with the warning | the length fits the content |

  The default target is 60 to 90 s. The best-measured Instagram band is 45 to 90 s (Socialinsider: about 6 million brand Reels, January to June 2026, median views highest at 45 to 60 s and 60 to 90 s next; brand accounts, correlational [SI-length]); on TikTok videos over 60 s led on reach in about 1.1 million videos, again correlational, and Buffer itself says the hook and quality matter more [BF-length]. The risk is dead time, not length.

#### P5c. The caption family: six styles, a rule for changing them, pt-BR chunking (D24, D26, D27)
Today's look is one style: one to three words, Inter 800, the spoken word lifted and coloured. The gap is range, not quality. The tools reviewed use one style per project (VEED states it [VEED-support]); changing the style at a beat boundary is rare there and is this plan's chance to be better, and its risk.
- **The family.** One parameter set is exposed (font role, case, colour roles, active-word treatment, stroke, shadow, chunk size, position), not a long theme list. Sizes are on 1080x1920; **every value is the research's starting estimate, tuned on real renders like A4's values.**

  | Style | Used for | Chunk | Size | Active word |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Pulse (default, today's look) | the base | 1 to 3 words, at most 22 characters | 96 to 132 px | `accent` plus a lift, 180 ms |
  | Block | hook and claims | 1 to 2 words, at most 14 characters | 140 to 200 px, Pulse below 110 px | whole chunk white, one emphasis word `accent` |
  | Sweep | dense explanation | up to 2 lines of at most 26 characters, all words visible | 64 to 80 px | a box slides to the next word, 80 ms; later words dim |
  | Quiet | devotional, calm | one line, 4 to 5 words, at most 28 characters | 72 to 88 px | colour cross-fade 120 ms, no movement |
  | Type | quotes, verses, terms | one line, at most 26 characters, in a chip | 60 to 72 px, mono or text | characters revealed across 80% of the spoken duration |
  | Stack | the payoff | 3 to 5 words over 2 lines | 88 px, the emphasis word 1.5x | words enter on a 100 ms stagger; one emphasis word |
  - Entrances are pure functions of `t`, at most 140 ms for Pulse and Block, under half the chunk length; exits are instant cuts; the stroke stays 0.12 to 0.14 em. The hold after speech is 0.5 s for Sweep (Netflix's rule, [NF-timing]).
- **The base style is a project setting (D5; principle 5).** A field of the `media` key of `runtime.json` (the package names it; recommended `caption_style`), default **Pulse**, set from the terminal like the other media settings; Pulse, Quiet and Sweep are base choices. The caption review (A4) shows the style in force and the person can see it before the render. The person decides the register of a video, not a model.
- **Which style, by beat role** (the script's `role` decides it, `align` applies it). One priority order, highest first: **`hook` > a quoted verse or `quote` preset > `payoff` > `cta` > the base style.**
  - `hook` takes Block; a beat with a `quote` preset or a quoted span takes Type; `payoff` takes Stack; `cta` takes Block; everything else takes the base. The research's Sweep-for-long-explanation rule is dropped: Sweep is a base choice.
  - **Constraints:** a style changes only at a beat boundary; a changed style lasts until the first beat boundary at or after 8 s from its start (so a hook of about 3 s keeps Block across the next boundary, and the higher priority wins inside that window); a style that is still on when the video ends needs no minimum; **at most 3 distinct styles, the base included. Over the cap, the lowest priorities fall back to the base style**, in the order above.
  - **A Block caption and the `title` or a `hook` preset on screen at once:** the caption yields. For that span the caption is drawn in the base style in the free band, so no words are lost and two large texts never compete; the title repeats the spoken key phrase (rule 2).
  - `align` writes `caption_styles[]` (a style from the closed list and a word range by index; numbers and enums only) into `beats.json`, renderer-neutral (D21). `base` and the edit list are not involved.
- **Emphasis words** (Pulse, Block, Stack) are picked by **closed predicates only**, in this priority: a digit token or a number word of the language list; a word of the language's negation list; a word inside a quoted span. At most one per chunk and at most 25% of chunks (the research's inference between two published densities). **The cap wins:** when the picks exceed it, the lower priorities, then the later ones, are dropped; a Stack chunk left without a pick is drawn as Pulse. The person edits the list in the caption review (stored as word indices); the person's edits are not capped and are listed beside the cap. Which word sells the point needs taste, so the person reviews it.
- **pt-BR chunking and the glyph test.** Chunk by characters first, then by words (Portuguese runs 15 to 25% longer than English, per a translation-industry source of unclear method [PT-length]). A word over 14 characters always stands alone; a number token ("3,5%", "R$ 1.500") and a hyphenated clitic are never split; a word under 0.25 s merges into its neighbour chunk. **Sentence case is the default**, because capitals put the tilde, circumflex and cedilla above the cap line: line height at least 1.15, the stroke never clipped by the stage. All caps only in Block and only if the glyph test passes. **The glyph test** renders "AÇÃO, CORAÇÃO, ÊXITO, PÃO" and the same in lowercase in every style and font and fails on a missing glyph or on clipping. Whether Inter's allowed subset covers the accents is inferred from its Unicode range and not verified. (validate: allow english-only -- the Portuguese examples are the subject of this line)
- **Caption spelling (D24):** captions follow speech, as the transcript has them ("pra", "tá", "a gente"); the person corrects them in the caption review (A4). Code does not rewrite spoken forms into standard spelling. (validate: allow english-only -- the Portuguese examples are the subject of this line)
- **Caption band (D26):** its bottom goes from 1620 to 1560 px, to clear the apps' interface. The margins are third-party and disagree on the bottom edge; neither platform publishes a safe-zone document that was found, so the value is **checked on a phone in each app**, not assumed.
- **Fonts (D27):** Anton (SIL OFL) joins the allow-list for Block only, with its licence file, hash and a glyph test; Inter and JetBrains Mono stay. Google Fonts lists Western European coverage for Anton [AN-font]; ç and ã are inferred from its Unicode range and tested. (validate: allow english-only -- the Portuguese examples are the subject of this line)
- **Changes:** the caption template and the style table (sizes, timings, easing) in `renderer-presets.json`, **not** in `video-presets.json`, so that a style change does not age the skill's evidence; `beats.json`'s schema (`caption_styles[]`); `_align.py` (the style per range); the allow-list; the `media` setting; P4b and P7's review view (the emphasis list and the base style). **Hard limits unchanged:** the determinism lint, the zones check and text hardening apply to every style.
- **Tests (offline):** each style at its sizes with the glyph string, in a fixture that fails when a glyph is missing; the style-by-role rule over synthetic scripts (the priority order, the 8 s window, the cap of 3 styles and the fall-back to the base, the Block caption yielding to a title); emphasis picks under the 25% cap, a Stack chunk without a pick drawn as Pulse; chunking with numbers, long words and clitics; easing equal at equal `t`; the same sha256 at the core counts Docker reports (a few distinct values up to its CPU count, read at run time, never hard-coded).

#### P5d. Motion: richer presets, new presets, and the framing layer
The overlay pages are transparent. A zoom, a dim or a transition changes the video itself, so **it belongs to an ffmpeg-side framing layer**, not to a page. Timings below are the research's estimates; no source measured them. There is no measured data for the faith, tech-career or communication niches, or for pt-BR audiences.
- **Richer existing presets** (each new look is a `choice` with a default in the parameter contract, so a cheap model stays safe):
  - `keyword`: an underline drawn on 300 ms after the pop settles (or a highlight bar), an `entrance` choice (pop, slam, word by word) and a slow drift (1.00 to 1.03 over the hold) so the card is never frozen;
  - `list`: the tick is drawn when the item is **spoken** (the word times are known), with a counter that advances and completed items dimmed;
  - `number`: a settle kick when the count ends, an optional delta arrow, and the exact final value always;
  - `pip`: a scale-in with overshoot and a ring drawn as a stroke;
  - `quote` and `cutaway` follow later in the same way (a word-by-word reveal; a named focus region with a drawn marker).
- **New presets, ranked** (the preset file grows, so the parameter contracts and the tests grow with it): 1 `hook` (an opening title in the top third, about 4 words, from the `title` of P3b, opaque at frame 0; **the `hook` and `compare` presets come from P5d, and P3b's rule 7 and the title's first frame depend on them**); 2 `receipt` (a highlighted screenshot: an asset the person uploads (D19), a `focus` from a closed list of nine regions, a `mark` of box, underline or dim); 3 `step` (a numbered or chapter marker, index, total and a label of at most 24 characters; a progress bar only on numbered videos); 4 `sticker` (a closed SVG set drawn from an icon set **still to be chosen under D6**; recommended Lucide, ISC [LUCIDE-licence]; no colour emoji); 5 `compare` (before and after, or myth and truth, two short texts); 6 `myth` (a struck-through claim, then the truth). Lowest value: a `transition` marker, off-tone for part of the audience.
- **The framing layer, on the ffmpeg side, in the edit list as enums and bounded numbers (`framing.punches[]`, D21):**
  - **Punch-in:** a hard cut to a zoom of 110 to 115%, returned by a cut, planned from sentence boundaries and from `emphasis: punch`, about one per 10 s. One guide cut eleven punches in 30 s to three, each on a key claim [AS-reels]. **It is drawn with crop and scale and the bit-exact scaler flags, not `zoompan`**, so that the output stays byte-identical. **The total zoom of any frame, A4's zoom on cut and push-in and a punch together, is at most 1.15**, a bounded number in the schema with a test; the earlier 120% "strong" setting is dropped. It adds to A4's zoom and does not replace it. The zones check uses the face box as moved by the zoom of that frame (A4's rule). Whether a crop of that size looks soft on the person's source is on A4's list to measure.
  - **Dim:** the speaker darkened to about 60% brightness over 200 ms under a card or a receipt. **No blur** until the same frame hashes equal twice and across the container.
  - **A pacing budget** (estimates, a checklist and not a law): an event about every 3 to 4 s in the first 10 s and every 5 to 8 s after; 6 to 12 overlay beats per 60 s; cutaways of 2 to 4 s; never two big events inside 400 ms.
- **Under K (S1, done):** the overlay presets are written here from this specification, with count-up, the highlight box and the slam as ideas and no copied file; the framing layer is ffmpeg code.
- **Tests (offline):** the contract of every new preset (types, bounds, defaults, the template and the declared parameters equal); a punch moves the zones check; the total zoom never exceeds 1.15 over the schema's bounds; no two big events within 400 ms in the planner; every filter keeps the byte-identical output at the core counts Docker reports (read at run time, not hard-coded); a preset whose asset is missing fails the check.
- **Class:** provider, **no lab test** (the preset file ages P3b's evidence, see its class). **Depends on:** S1, done with outcome K, for the overlay presets; P5c-0 for `emphasis`; none for the framing layer. **The person approves the preset list (the richer looks and the ranked new presets) before it freezes (D5; the gate C2 set for the first list), and (amendment A6) approves it from the catalogue: each new preset adds a `samples.<preset>` entry in `renderer-presets.json` and a `use_when` in the vocabulary file, a test fails without them, and P5f lands before that approval**, and the lab test of P3b waits for that freeze. **Done when:** the person has approved the list, and a render of the first real recording shows richer presets and punch-ins that the person judges against the "too simple" verdict.

#### P5e. Sound cues, later (D25)
After the visual work (P5c and P5d done). A new capability: audio mixing on the ffmpeg side.
- A fixed cue per preset, never a script choice: a pop on the frame the element reaches rest, a whoosh starting 3 to 6 frames before a move, a soft tick per list item. At most one cue per beat, ducked under the voice, never two on one beat. The offsets are the researcher's own practice values, not sourced. **The cue table lives in `renderer-presets.json`**, the renderer's own file, not in `video-presets.json`, so a change to it does not age `mkt-social-copy`'s evidence.
- **A small pack of 10 to 20 files from CC0 sources** (Kenney first, per its listing as CC0 [KN-audio]; a CC0 filter on Freesound), each with a hash and a source line in an allow-list like the fonts'. **Kenney's own licence page is read before any of its files is added** (only a third-party listing was read so far). Files under the Pixabay, Mixkit and LottieFiles terms are fine inside a video and are **never committed**, because those terms forbid redistributing the files as such.
- **Determinism.** A new audio generation is a new place where bytes can drift: the S1 run found HyperFrames' AAC packet tables differing between runs with identical decoded audio. P5e carries a test that the finished file has the same sha256 over repeated runs and at the core counts Docker reports, and **normalises the AAC packet tables** (decode to WAV, encode from that, mux with the copied video) if the test finds a difference. Principle 11: the approval binds the bytes.
- The 2026 trend sources disagree on sound, and the research found the loud style described as saturated for serious niches (opinion). The default for a project is a mood setting the person chooses (none or subtle); recommended: none until the person asks.
- **Class:** provider, no lab test. **Done when** a cue plays on a render, its level is under the voice by a measured margin the package states, and the determinism test passes.

#### A5.4 What not to copy
- Screen shake, glitch and light leaks: off-tone for faith content (an inference); and heavy whooshes.
- Loud all-caps captions throughout, random rotation, 3D flips, constant bounce on every word, handwritten fonts for continuous speech, colour emoji fonts (not deterministic) and one-word captions for the whole video.
- Cuts every 1.5 s everywhere, a zoom on every line, a spin transition between every clip, effects stacked on one beat.
- Vendor statistics as rules (uplift in retention, hook rates, "a pattern interrupt every 4 s"): no checkable sample. They are things to test.
- Comment-to-DM automation: an external tool and a side effect this plan does not control; the script may say "comment X" as plain text.

- GSAP in the core; Remotion for now (its licence probably puts a product that renders for users on a paid plan, an inference from its terms, to be confirmed with the vendor [RM-licence]); text behind the speaker by matting; dozens of themed caption identities.
- Files from Pixabay, Mixkit or LottieFiles in the repository.

## 7. Security and limits

- **Credentials never inside a run.** The publisher, transcriber and renderer run in code on the host (the transcriber's engine and the renderer's engine each inside a container the provider starts, which receives no credential and no host environment). Their credentials go through `providers/secrets/resolver.py`; the transcriber has none. In M1 no run sees any of the recording. In M2 a run sees only the transcript and the post file (L2, L6), and only when the project's consent setting is on (D17). A hosted transcriber, if chosen (D4), is a provider with its key in the registry, never in a run, and the same consent setting gates it.
- **The media routes are loopback-only.** The upload route and `media-raw` answer on the loopback socket and not on the `--lan` socket, as `GET /token-file` does. With `--lan` the token and `/api` cross the network as plain HTTP (`runtime/service.py:190-194`), and a recording or a render must not. The phone gets the video to the Mac by AirDrop or cable.
- **The transcriber runs in a container with hard limits (amendment A3, D20).** The recording is mounted read-only into a container with no network, a read-only root, a size-bound tmpfs, dropped capabilities, no host environment, CPU, memory and process limits, and a timeout after which the container is killed by name. The model is mounted read-only after its sha256 is checked against the value pinned in the provider's data. One output folder is read-write and its content is read as untrusted. The provider reads no credential. The raw recording still never leaves the machine. Each limit has a named offline test with a stub for docker (P4). Stated limits: apt packages in the image are not pinned by version; the image is not assumed byte-identical across machines, so its id is recorded in each transcript's facts; the model file could be swapped between the check and the mount (the folder is the person's, 0700).
- **The renderer runs in a container with hard limits (D22, approved 2026-10-13).** The image is built from a Dockerfile the workbench owns: `debian:bookworm-slim` pinned by digest, Debian snapshot packages, Chrome for Testing pinned by version and archive sha256. The container has no network, a read-only root, dropped capabilities, `no-new-privileges`, a non-root user, no host environment, CPU, memory and process limits, read-only mounts for the recording, the assets, the fonts and the JSON inputs, and one read-write output folder whose content is read as untrusted; the host kills it by name past the time limit. The host runs only code that validates inputs, runs the container and reads the result. **Chrome's sandbox inside the container is a stated trade-off:** a seccomp profile that allows it, or `--no-sandbox` with the container as the boundary; the package chooses once, records it in each render's facts, and never falls back silently. Stated limits: the image is not byte-identical across machines (its id is recorded) and the host container runtime and its VM are a trust base. Each limit has a named offline test with a stub for docker (P5).
- **Downloads are the person's word; nothing is downloaded by an agent without it.** Building the image (`--build`, started by the person) downloads `ubuntu:22.04`, apt packages and the whisper.cpp source at the pinned tag. The model files (`fetch-model`, started by the person, or put in the folder by hand) come from the official whisper.cpp model location on Hugging Face, each checked against its pinned sha256. The renderer image (its `--build`, started by the person) downloads `debian:bookworm-slim` by digest, the Debian snapshot packages and the pinned Chrome for Testing archive, checked against its sha256 (P5). No pipeline step, task or agent runs a `--build` or `fetch-model`; a missing piece makes the task report it and wait.
- **The approval binds the exact bytes, and the person sees what is bound.**
  - The effect document holds the rendered video's sha256, and **the caption text itself with its sha256**, as the pull-request kind embeds its title and body. The person approves the document's hash (`effects.py:202-221`, `ops.py:727-729`).
  - The card renders from the hashed document, not from a file read separately. `media-raw` for a card takes the pending decision's id, hashes the **open file descriptor** against the bound `video_sha256`, and streams from that same descriptor; a mismatch is refused.
  - Renders are **content-addressed and immutable**: `render-<sha12>.mp4`, created with `O_EXCL`, mode 0400, never rewritten.
  - The document holds every Instagram option the request sends (`media_type`, `share_to_feed`, `thumb_offset`) with an explicit value. The publisher sends no parameter that is not in its argv, and a test compares the request the dry run prints with the request sent. The card shows the cover frame (or "cover: first frame"), the @mentions separately, and the slot time with its offset.
  - The scheduler copies the approved files into the job and re-hashes them at the slot (`providers/scheduler/README.md`).
  - The publisher's ledger refuses the same key with other bytes, as LinkedIn's does (`linkedin.py:504-506`), and records the container id before `media_publish`.
  - A re-render is a new file, a new hash and a new approval.
  - **Code opens the effect, never a manifest gate** (`OPENED_BY = "operation"`, P8), so a scheduled text post of `mkt-publish` is not routed to this kind.
- **What a model may never do unattended:**
  - schedule or publish (only code, after an approval of the exact hash);
  - choose a TikTok privacy level (the person, every time [TT-guidelines]);
  - write a filter graph, a command, a path or a URL into the edit list (the validator refuses);
  - write a time or a caption text into the edit list: an edit list from a model carries only indices, preset names, typed parameter values (never a time) and short labels, and code derives times and captions from the transcript; a clip's in and out times are the person's (C1);
  - **set any option outside the closed list** (`share_to_feed`, `trial_params`, `collaborators`, `user_tags`, `location_id` and `audio_name` are never set by a model);
  - **re-render or re-open an effect without the person**;
  - **purge before a terminal status** (the scheduler's `purge` refuses it);
  - **choose the slot time**: it comes from the post file and is shown with its offset;
  - **edit the transcript's text or times, or remove a retake** (code proposes the retakes, the person confirms them and edits the text, and code owns every time; no model is involved in the caption review);
  - add music (out of scope; copyright);

  - **The consent switch (D17).** In M2 the edit run reads the transcript, outtakes included, and a run reaches the model provider (`AGENTS.md:145`).
