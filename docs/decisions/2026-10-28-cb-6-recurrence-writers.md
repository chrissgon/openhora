## 2026-10-28: both calendar writers write, read and cancel a daily or weekly series, and cancel one occurrence (CB-6, RECUR-WRITERS; limits L57, L58; no model call, no provider call, no lab run, fingerprint unchanged)


`SPEC-CATALOG-BATCH` section 6.6, decided by the maintainer on 2026-10-27 ("Criar e cancelar", OPEN-CB-14; "Entra", OPEN-CB-23). The runtime side (cards, the which-one rule, the guards) is CB-7 and is not in this package. Builds on OUTLOOK-TZ (#588, merged): a series is written in the zone it is expanded in.

- **What it is.** A create spec may hold `recurrence` (`{"freq": "daily"}` or `{"freq": "weekly", "byday": "MO".."SU"}`, nothing else: no end, no interval, no day of the month) and `time_zone` (an IANA name) together, for a timed event whose weekday in that zone is the weekly day; a patch never carries either (`providers/_writer_calendar.py`: `read_spec`, `zone_name`, `window` and the other closed helpers, shared by both writers). Google writes `recurrence: ["RRULE:FREQ=DAILY"]` or `["RRULE:FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=MO"]` with `timeZone` on `start` and `end`; Outlook writes `recurrence.pattern` and `recurrence.range` (`noEnd`, `startDate` the date of the start in the zone) with `start` and `end` in the zone, and refuses (exit 2) a first wall time that is ambiguous in the zone rather than write a series in UTC. `event-get` prints `series_id` and `series_rule` (`daily`, `weekly:<MO..SU>`, `other`: the closed reading of the master's rule, which is `other` for an end, a count, an interval but 1, two days or any other line). `event-instances` reads the instances of a series in a window of at most two days (no title, no attendee). The whole series is cancelled by `event-delete --series` (Google: the existing DELETE pair, on the master's id) or `event-cancel-series` (Outlook); one occurrence by `event-cancel-occurrence` (Google: a PATCH of the instance with `status: cancelled` and the key property only, on the existing PATCH pair; Outlook: Graph's `cancel` action on the occurrence's id). Each of the three cancels reads the event again right before and refuses, exit 1 and nothing sent, a moved base, a series whose rule is `other`, one the person does not organise or with other attendees; the occurrence cancel also refuses an instance of another series, a moved one (`--expect-start`), a cancelled one and one that has started. No verb sends a DELETE but Google's series one.
- **Limits.** L57: no DELETE pair is added (Google's one is also the series', Outlook's two are unchanged); Outlook's cancel is a POST with an empty JSON object (no comment: no word of anyone reaches an attendee) and, if Graph refuses it, the writer sends nothing else: there is NO fallback to a DELETE, and the maintainer's S21 exception is not extended to a series. L58: the allow-lists gain only Google's `GET .../events/{id}/instances` and Outlook's two `GET .../events/{id}/instances` and two `POST .../events/{id}/cancel`; `runtime/tests/test_limits_account_writes.py` gains a measure that names the cancel path only in those two pairs (method POST) and their path builder. Every write keeps `--confirmed` with the dry-run hash.
- **Facts read on the vendors' pages on 2026-10-27, and what they do not say.** Google events resource (https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/events): `start.timeZone` and `end.timeZone` "for recurring events this field is required and specifies the time zone in which the recurrence is expanded", DTSTART and DTEND are not allowed in `recurrence`, an instance carries `recurringEventId` and `originalStartTime`; the recurring-events guide (https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/recurringevents): an instance is cancelled by setting `status` to `cancelled` with a PUT to its URL (a PATCH of `status` alone is NOT read there: to verify live); `events.instances` (https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/events/instances): `timeMin` filters on the end, `timeMax` is exclusive on the start, `showDeleted` defaults to false (it lists no instance field); `events.delete` and `events.patch` (read) say nothing of a series, of an instance or of the trash: what deleting a master's id does to its instances and where it goes is to verify live. Graph `recurrencePattern` (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/recurrencepattern) requires `firstDayOfWeek` for a weekly pattern (the spec had left it out; the writer sends the documented default, `sunday`), `recurrenceRange` (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/recurrencerange): `startDate` "must be the same value as the start property", `recurrenceTimeZone` "if not specified, the time zone of the event is used"; `event: cancel` (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/event-cancel): 202, "moves the event to the Deleted Items folder", an occurrence is cancelled by its id, an attendee gets 400, the body holds an optional `comment`, the page speaks of meetings (an event with no attendee, and a series master's id, are to verify live); `List instances` (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/event-list-instances): required `startDateTime` and `endDateTime`, UTC unless `Prefer: outlook.timezone`, each item has `type` `occurrence` or `exception`, `seriesMasterId` and an `@odata.etag` (the writer prints the `changeKey` as `etag`, the base its other verbs compare). All of these are the maintainer's live step 4b (`providers/writer/README.md`, "To verify live").
- **Choices where the spec left one.** (1) `--series` is a switch on Google's `event-delete` and carries the master's id on `event-instances` and `event-cancel-occurrence`; the occurrence cancel takes `--series` and `--expect-start` (the spec's checks need what the document holds, and its flag list had neither): the dry-run hash covers the request, so CB-7 passes both again on the confirmed run. (2) The Graph cancel body is `{}`, not none (the page requires a Content-Type and a JSON object whose only member is an optional comment). (3) A series is a timed event: `recurrence` with `all_day` true is exit 2. (4) `time_zone` is a spec key for a series; Outlook's `--time-zone` flag of #588 stays for a plain event, and beside a series must be the same name (else exit 2). (5) The occurrence cancel also refuses a series of other attendees or someone else's (the spec lists it for the whole series; Graph's cancel tells attendees). (6) Google's plain `event-delete` (no `--series`) reads the event right before and refuses, exit 1 and nothing sent, one that is part of a series (a master or an instance) or that is gone, as Outlook's does (the Opus review of this package asked for it: the one DELETE pair is then reached only for a series master by `--series` and for an event that is no part of a series), at the cost of one GET in every Google removal. (7) The dry run's `would` holds `verb` and `guard` besides the request, so the hash the person approves covers the flags that decide which checks run (a different verb on the same id, or other `--series` or `--expect-start`, is another preview); the request is unchanged. (8) `build()` has an explicit branch for the one verb that builds the DELETE and raises for any other verb. Not done, logged by the supervisor: `event-patch` on a master's id moves the whole series with no writer check.
