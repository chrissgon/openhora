## 2026-10-28: T8-3: the mail tests assert phrase keys and fields, not quoted sentences (SPEC-V3-STRUCTURE section 5; tests only; no model call, no provider call, no lab run, fingerprint unchanged)

The third family of T8 after the kit (T8-0a): `test_send_*` and `test_mail_*` (15 files; 8 with a sentence to convert). A sentence of the phrase table that a test quoted is now `pk.said(text, "KEY", cfg, field=value)` (or `pk.assert_said`, or `assert_lines` for the lines of a card); no test was removed or merged and no product file changed.

- **Counts.** 86 assert statements changed in 8 files (an assert counted once when its text differs from the one at the base): `test_send_own_account.py` 32, `test_mail_revise_flow.py` 16, `test_mail_read.py` 12, `test_mail_compose_flow.py` 10, `test_mail_revise_gate.py` 7, `test_mail_refresh.py` 4, `test_mail_reply_compose_flow.py` 4, `test_mail_numbers_seed.py` 1. The spec's scan (fragments of 14 characters or more) found 55 here; the rest are card labels (`Subject: `, `To: `, `Account: `), refusals quoted by a fragment (`not set up for this project`, `may not read it`, `is a message of another account than`), module constants (`FAILED`, `RECIPIENTS`, `ALREADY`) and the pt-BR sentences of the reviser.
- **Same helpers, one place.** `test_send_own_account.py` (the base of the mail family, imported by the others) has `CFG`, `PT` and `assert_lines(lines, [(key, fields), ...], cfg)` (each line says its sentence, in order, and there are no other lines); `test_mail_compose_flow.py` has `has(lines, key, **fields)`; `test_mail_revise_flow.py` has `says(text, key)` (the whole text is the one pt-BR sentence) and `reply_is(got, key, resend)` (the `{"say": ..., "resend": ...}` pair of a revision); `test_mail_read.py` has `told(text, (key, fields), ...)`, because a reply of one line says several sentences joined by a space (the question of two boxes and how to name one), which `said` cannot find inside a line.
- **A refusal with a prefix.** An error text can carry words the code adds before or after the table sentence (`bcc: `, `prepare: refused account -- `, `error: `, ` Nothing was sent.`): the converted assert cuts the added words off (`str(error).partition(" -- ")[2]`) and asserts the table sentence with its fields; the cut is written in the assert.
- **Kept literal.** `test_send_email.py` has nothing to convert: the outbox card (`send_document.body` of a non-own e-mail) is written by code, not read from the table, so its lines (`Subject: ...`, `... not on this card ...`) coincide with a table sentence by accident and pinning them to the table would assert something the code does not do. The rest: texts of errors that are not in the table (`is not enabled for this channel`, `approved in the terminal`, `nothing was sent`), the group text of `operations.GROUP_TEXT` (code), the rows of a list, and the lines a person types. Two constants stay as strings because a file of another family imports them: `FAILED` of `test_mail_revise_flow.py` (`test_turn_gate_admit.py`); the asserts of this file use `FAILED_KEY`.
- **Vacuity (sampled, planted in the product code and put back).** A key switched (`RV_LOST` answered as `RV_FAILED`, `ops_card_revise.py:225`): the test of a new card that cannot be opened fails. A field changed (`CARD_MAIL_COUNT` plus one, `send_document.py:478`): 2 tests fail. A field changed (`MR_WHICH_BOX` count plus one, `ops_mail_read.py:347`): 2 tests fail. A wording changed in both languages (`CARD_MAIL_SEND`, `RV_ALREADY`): 226 tests still pass, as the spec asks; the snapshot that pins the wording (T8-0b) is not built yet.
- **What did not hold.** `said` is whole-line only, so a refusal with a prefix or a reply of one line with several sentences needed the cuts above (a kit form that finds a sentence inside a line would remove them: suggestion for the kit). The kit's `which` on two lines of the same template (`Subject: {subject}` exists three times in the table) cannot tell the keys apart, so the mail card lines are asserted per line with `said`, not through `which`. `architecture_tables.py --check` fails at the base of this branch, unrelated.
