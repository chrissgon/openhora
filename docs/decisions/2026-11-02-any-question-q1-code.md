## 2026-11-02: a question the interpreter reads is answered by code and changes nothing (ANY-QUESTION Q1; no model call, inert until the prompt asks for the kind)

**What this is.** The sixth kind of the interpreter's protocol, `question` (`about` `card`, `done`, `can` or `other`), and what code does with it. The kind is inert until the prompt asks the model for it (package Q3): no recorded answer of the repository is a `question`, so no golden row of `runtime/tests/test_entry_replay.py` moves (35 passed, 16 skipped). Nothing here calls a model or a provider, and no lab run was made.

**The transport of the waiting card (a thread slot).** A plain line after a card goes through the bridge's frame, `decision-card-revise`, `Bridge.say` and `Bridge.decide` to `Bridge.line`, which builds a NEW `Turn` for the stage `bridge-say`, so the frame's `Turn` is not handed on, and `runtime/tests/entry_kit.py` wraps `Bridge.say` with a fixed signature. Two ways to hand the number of the card to `ops_say.say` were measured on the replay of the golden rows: a `waiting_card` keyword of `run_op("say", ...)` changed 44 golden rows (the `ops_calls` of `run_op:say` gains the keyword, nothing else); a slot on the thread that runs the line (`runtime/line_card.py`, the reverse of `runtime/line_stage.py`) changed none. The slot was chosen: `Bridge.waiting_card` reads the conditions of `revise_waiting` again at the moment the line runs (the last message, a whole open card of this project, its hash unchanged, not on a platform whose acts are replies), `Bridge.line` holds the number around the one `run_op("say")` of a plain line (never the route leg and never a confirmed plan), and `ops_say.say` reads it into `extras["waiting_card"]`. `run_op` calls the operation in-process, so the thread is the same.

**The key names.** The design's `CARD_ASK_*` names are not used. The family `CARD_` is the family of card LINES (`phrases.CARD_FAMILY`, `card_valid`), so a sentence named `CARD_ASK_EVENT` would be accepted as a card line. The 17 keys are `QUESTION_CARD_SAYS` (the lines come from the card, whichever its kind), `QUESTION_CARD_APPROVE` (the card's own first line says what approving does), `QUESTION_CARD_NONE`, `QUESTION_NO_CARD`, `QUESTION_CARD_WORD`, `QUESTION_OTHER`, `CAN_YES`, `CAN_CARD` (added for `can` of `approve` or `reject`), `UNDERSTOOD_DONE`, `DONE_HEAD`, `DONE_LINE`, `DONE_NONE` and the five `DONE_STATE_*`. They live in the pair `runtime/phrases_parts/any-question.en.json` and `.pt-BR.json`.

**`op` is optional on `done`.** The design said `op` is present only with `done` or `can`; here it is optional with `done` ("was yesterday's card approved?" names no operation, and its right answer has none), required with `can`, absent with `card` and `other`. An `op` present must be an operation of the served catalog, the refused ones included (`approve` is accepted); the policy is not asked by the check, because a question does nothing, and `can` reads the policy when it answers.

**The three paths of `done`.** An op of the reminders (`remind`, `reminder-forget`, `reminder-restore`, `reminders`) is the plan of the existing read of the reminders; an op that is not an act of class E is the plan of the existing read of the state of the work (`status`); anything else (an act of a family, or no op) is the plan of `pending` with the question `{"op": op}` in its state, whose sentence is `UNDERSTOOD_DONE`, whose hash binds the question (`plan_sha256` carries `question_done` only when given, so every other plan's hash is byte-identical, held by a test), and whose OK, through `ops_say._confirmed`, lists the person's cards of the last 7 days of the family of the op (at most 3, newest first, how each ended: `ops_any_question.question_done`, `done_reply`). The reply keeps a neutral word in the conversation. "approved" says the decision was approved; whether its write landed is not read.

**OPEN-4, as decided on 2026-11-02.** A card word in question form ("sim?") with a card waiting, read by the model as `none` or as a question, decides nothing (limit L64): the person is told how to decide it (`QUESTION_CARD_WORD`) and the card is sent again. With no card waiting it stays `IX_NONE`.

**The card call.** `revise` answers a question `about` `card` from the card's own lines as the sender may read them (a private line the sender may not read is cut by `ops_grants.private_view`), sends the card again and ends, opens and remembers nothing; the admission is spent. Any other question passes on to the main turn, which reads it once through `ops_card_revise.decided`.

**The limit.** A question never opens, ends, approves, rejects or runs anything (limit L55): `runtime/tests/test_question_never_acts.py` runs every question the model can ask, with each of two waiting cards, and counts decisions, statuses, hashes, approvals, rejections, executes, requests and cards opened by writes in words, and the OK of every plan it leaves.

**The Portuguese was approved.** The 17 sentences of `any-question.pt-BR.json` are as the spec wrote them; the maintainer approved that text on 2026-11-02 (OPEN-9, answered "it is fine" in Portuguese).

**Not done here, on purpose.** The phrase snapshot (`runtime/tests/phrase_snapshot.py`) and the generated blocks (`scripts/architecture_tables.py`) are not regenerated by this package: the batch keeper does it. Both `--check` commands report exactly the 17 new keys and the two new modules, and the tests that read them pass in lenient mode.

**Two existing tests list the new modules.** `test_runtime_rules` (the `allowed` sets of `ops_say.py` and `ops_interpret.py` gain `ops_any_question`) and `test_turn_gate_refresh` (the expected set of files with a thread-local gains `line_card.py`, which holds a card number, not a refresh budget); approved by the supervisor.

**Tests of the mail card.** The tests hold a mail card as a stored effect decision with the lines of a real send card, written into the store of the calendar world, and not as one opened through the mail family (whose world cannot be combined with the calendar world in one module).
