## 2026-11-24: The HyperFrames runtime and GSAP in the common image, and the frame-exact retime (PF-a1; plan video quality v6)

**Context.** The second page contract (PF-a, `SPEC-video-PFa-second-contract-2026-11-24.md`) draws admitted HyperFrames pages in the ffmpeg engine. Those pages need GSAP and the HyperFrames runtime, which only the HyperFrames image carried. PF-a1 is the part that depends on nothing else: it puts them in the common image and fixes a one-frame lag in the ffmpeg engine's compose graph.

**Decision.**
- **The maintainer's words, 2026-11-24:** authorising the work, "Sim, comece (Recomendado)"; D19, baking GSAP and the runtime into the ffmpeg engine's image from the same locked source and version, never committed, under the same GSAP licence, "Sim, levar (Recomendado)"; on the trade, "Aceito (Recomendado)".
- The common image fetches, at build, `gsap.min.js` (gsap 3.14.2), `hyperframe.runtime.iife.js` and `hyperframe.manifest.json` (hyperframes 0.9.19) from the npm registry, pinned by the lockfile's sha512 and our sha256 (`hf-runtime/npm-pins.json`), and the registry's `LICENSE` at commit `b4cc674f` (`hf-runtime/registry.sha256`). They go to `/opt/renderer/vendor` and `/opt/renderer/hf-registry`, mode 0444, and are never committed. The fetch (`hf-runtime/fetch_hf.py`) comes before `COPY .` in the Dockerfile, so a code change does not fetch again. `_inner.py selfcheck` reads them, and the five shims, again (`vendor_hash`).
- The five shims (`early_stub`, `capture_mode_shim`, `virtual_time_shim`, `render_mode`, `bridge`) are committed from the pinned source; only the fps literal of `render_mode.js` is a placeholder (`__OH_FPS__`).
- **The retime (R3-11).** `compose_argv` retimes the concat's output once, `settb=1/<fps>,setpts=N`, before the picture splits into the plain path and the crop path, and the split's crop and mask use the same. `setpts=N/(fps*TB)` on a stream in the microsecond time base truncates, and the speaker's crop landed a frame late at an edge whose number is not divisible by three at 30 fps. The test `test_the_speaker_mask_and_crop_meet_on_edges_not_divisible_by_three` (edges 31 and 92 over two kept ranges, a beat marker starting on 37) failed on unchanged main (frame 90: the crop showed the frame before the picture beside it) and passes now. `base_graph` is not touched, so the HyperFrames engine's check reads the same graph. The pip and clip layers keep their own retime (they are streams on their own clock, not the concat's output).

**What it saves and what it loses (principle 13).** It saves the HyperFrames engine's separate image for every page that needs GSAP or the runtime: the second contract can draw them in the ffmpeg engine's container, with no second image and no second browser. It loses: the common image grows by about 1 MiB and its tag changes (everyone rebuilds once, D19); a build now needs the npm registry and one raw-file URL (both pinned; a changed archive fails the build); every ffmpeg-engine render's bytes change (the retime, and possibly the mp4's timescale), so earlier renders are not reproducible byte for byte.

**Verified.** Offline: the pins equal the lockfile; the shims hash as pinned; `fetch_hf.py` passes a fake tarball and refuses a wrong integrity, a wrong sha256, a link, `../x`, an absolute path, an oversize member or tarball and a url outside the pattern, leaving no folder; selfcheck refuses a different, missing or extra vendor, registry or shim file; the compose goldens changed only by the `[cvt]` label and the retime (the diff is in the pull request); the new retime test and the host-ffmpeg split tests pass.

**Not verified here.** That the image builds and `selfcheck` passes with the real tarballs, and a render in the image with the frame-exact edges checked with `ffprobe` (the supervisor's step 2); that the mp4's timescale or the render's bytes beyond the retime did not change otherwise; that GSAP and the runtime draw anything (PF-a2 and later).
