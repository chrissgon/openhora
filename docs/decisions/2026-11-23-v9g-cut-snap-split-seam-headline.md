## 2026-11-23: Beat edges snap to cuts, the split's soft seam with the caption on it, the keyword's headline looks and the zoom spacing (V9g; renderer only, no skill bumped)

**Why.** The maintainer reviewed a real 9:16 talking-head reel against a reference creator's reels and asked for four changes. (1) An `aside` beat ended at output frame 117 while the cut was at frame 125: the picture flashed back to colour for 8 frames before the cut. (2) `side: top` (picture on top, speaker below: the reference pattern) was dropped as `split_no_room` for every face in the upper half of the frame, and `side: bottom` put the captions over the picture. (3) The keyword, a small bordered pill above the head that pops in, reads weak next to the reference's large stacked words over the chest. (4) The zoom changed too often and too close together, which read as a bug: on the reel `alternate` flipped at every cut (4.17 s in, 12.5 out, 17.67 in, 26.93 out, 32.7 in, 34.77 out), a 2.07 s range was drawn at 1.1, and a punch that ended at 26.65 s was followed by the cut's level change 0.28 s later.

**Decision 1: beat edges snap to the cuts of the output timeline.** `_edl.cut_frames` gives the cuts: the first output frame of every kept range after the first (`kept` is in source frames; the output position is the running length of the ranges before). `_edl.snap_to_cuts` runs in `resolve_timeline` after the one-track rule and the dropped beats: a start at most `SNAP_START_SECONDS` (0.25 s) after a cut moves back to it, and a beat before it that runs past the cut ends on the cut (the one-track rule), unless that would leave the earlier beat with no frame; then an end at most `SNAP_END_SECONDS` (0.5 s) before a cut moves forward to it, never past the next beat's start nor the video's end. The aside's grey (its mono grade rows) and its echo are computed from the snapped span, so they move with it. The review's `mono` and `tint` grades snap to the edges of their own kept range the same way, within `GRADE_SECONDS_MAX`; a flash or a dip sits on its word and never moves. A snapped edge is marked `snapped_start` / `snapped_end` on the beat. Cost: a beat may run up to 0.5 s past its preset's maximum and start up to 0.25 s before its first word; the end window is the longer because holding a beat past its words is invisible, starting one before its word is not. On the renderer kit's fictional post every beat now starts on a cut, which is the look asked for; the payload rows golden of `test_renderer_common.py` moved for that reason only (with the snap left out it is the V9d golden byte for byte). Both engines resolve the same timeline, so the HyperFrames engine snaps too: the clip cutaway goldens (`golden/hf-clip-cover.html`, `hf-clip-glass.html`, `hf-cutaway-picture.sha256`) moved by their timing only (the cutaway starts on the cut at frame 683, 3 frames before its word), and `test_pip_card.py` compares the frame before the card with the render without the card, since a snapped card may now start where the beat before it ends.

**Decision 2: a top or bottom split has a soft seam and its caption is a chip on the seam.** For `top` and `bottom`, `_layout.split_geometry` now returns `feather` (`split.feather_px`, 100) and `seam: true`, and its caption band is `split.seam_band_height` (150) rows centred on the seam, moved into the picture's half when it would come within `gap` of the moved face, and None only when it would then leave [`frame.safe.top`, `zones.caption_bottom`] (no top or bottom split with a face box inside the frame meets that). The page draws the picture over its own half, opaque, and carries it `feather` rows past the seam over the speaker with a linear mask (opaque at the seam, clear at the feather's end). This composes correctly with the compose order of `_media.compose_argv`: the speaker's crop is laid onto the base picture inside the speaker's half only, and the beat layer (this page) is above it, so the fade shows the crop and never the uncropped picture; a feather that straddled the seam would have shown the uncropped picture through the picture's half, so it lies entirely in the speaker's half. A caption piece inside such a split (`_layout.caption_pieces`) is marked `seam`, takes the seam band, and is checked against the moved face only: the picture covers the face where it was, so the moved face is the only face on screen. `caption_data` passes `seam` to the page, and the caption page draws the piece as a compact chip, centred on the band: the words on one line in the theme's background colour on the accent fill, as big as fits the band and the frame's width, a hard cut, the chip itself the measured element (`data-zone`), so the driver's face check and bottom-line check cover its whole box. The label chip moves to the corner away from the seam: the top-left of the picture's half for `top` (top at `frame.safe.top`), the bottom-left for `bottom` (its bottom on `zones.caption_bottom`). `left` and `right` keep the V9f behaviour (the band that avoids both faces, the opaque bar, the union of the faces). The face of the maintainer's case, {x 220, y 320, w 380, h 540}, now gives `top` the band [885, 1035] and a moved face at y 1170: kept. Alternative rejected: keeping the band inside the picture's half and only feathering, which leaves `top` with no band for a face in the upper half (the original face box covers that half).

**Decision 3: the keyword has two headline looks, picked per video; the pill stays the default.** `presets.keyword.variants` in `renderer-presets.json` holds `headline` and `headline-serif`; the edit list's new optional key `looks` ({preset: variant}, written by `base --look keyword=headline` or `--look keyword=headline-serif`, also taken by `storyboard`, checked by `_edl.check_looks`, `bad_look` otherwise) picks one for the whole video. `_payload.look_of` lays the variant over `presets.<preset>.looks` and `beat_data` gives that look to the page, so a video that names no look draws what it drew. The headline: the keyword's own words in uppercase, 2 to 4 stacked lines (one for a single word), as wide as 90 percent of the frame, the last line 1.2 times larger, in the accent colour (or the beat's `color`), no box and no border, a soft dark shadow, at the top of the caption band under the face; the words are split into the lines that give the largest type, measured at 100 px. It starts on its matched word with no lead (`lead_seconds` 0, the pill keeps its 0.05 s), neither pops nor fades, ends on a hard cut (on a cut when decision 1 applies), and replaces the caption on its frames (`replaces_caption`: `_layout.caption_gaps` cuts each caption chunk at the headline's edges and drops the pieces under it). The stack is the measured element, in the `caption` zone, with a margin of 0.22 em above its first line and 0.12 em below its last for accents and descenders inside that box, so the driver's face check and the 1560 bottom line hold for every glyph; the type is fitted to the band's height, so it never crosses the face box. `headline` uses the theme's display font (Inter 800 in practice). `headline-serif` uses a look font: a new list `look_fonts` in `assets/fonts/allowlist.json`, read by `_assets.look_font_allowlist` and never by a theme role, served as the page's `--font-look-keyword` by `_assets.load_look_fonts` with its sha256 checked like a theme font; a look whose font has no row is drawn with the look's fallback stack (`"Playfair Display", "DejaVu Serif", Georgia, serif`) and the render's `fonts` says `look-keyword: system default`, the allow-list's existing fallback rule. The vocabulary is unchanged (the keyword's text stays at most 24 characters), so `mkt-social-copy` and its checker are not touched and nothing is bumped. The HyperFrames engine refuses an edit list with `looks` (`look_not_on_engine`): its default keyword block is `hf:spring-pop`, its image carries only the three theme fonts, and its gate admits only their families. Why the pill stays the default: changing it would change every existing video's keyword and the HyperFrames goldens; the maintainer picks the look per video.

**Decision 4: the zoom level changes at most every `ZOOM_SPACING_SECONDS` (3 s).** `_media.zoomed_ranges(zoom, kept, fps)` picks the ranges `alternate` draws at `factor` from the whole list of kept ranges: the level flips at a cut, starting unzoomed, only when the cut is at least the spacing after the last change of level (the video's start counts as one) and the range itself lasts at least the spacing; otherwise the range keeps the level before it and the alternation goes on from there. `_media.zoom_levels` takes the whole list (`kept`) and every renderer caller passes it: the compose (`base_graph`, with `kept_all` when a plan holds one range, as the pixel check's reference does), `_edl.range_faces` and so `span_face` (the face boxes the cards and captions avoid), `resolve_punches`, the HyperFrames page and the gate's explanation; so the picture and the face check use the same levels. Called without the list it keeps the rule before V9g (every second range). Punches: a punch whose end falls at most `SNAP_END_SECONDS` (0.5 s) before its range's end, when a cut follows, ends on the cut, so the zoom changes once there; a punch that would start less than the spacing after a change of level (a cut where the base level changes, or an earlier punch's end) is dropped, reported `punch_spacing` beside `punch_short` and `punch_capped` (the runtime's closed list `EFFECT_REASONS` gains it). Dropped rather than delayed: a punch stresses its word, and a delayed one would land on another. The slow push-in is unchanged. On the reel's edit list the zoom becomes 4.17 s in, 12.5 out, 17.67 in, 26.93 out, 34.77 in: the 2.07 s range from 32.7 s keeps 1.0 and the level next changes at 34.77 s (7.8 s after the last change), and the punch of beat 6 runs 24.17 s to 26.93 s, ending on the cut. Cost: a punch within 3 s after a zoom change is no longer drawn. Tests of the zoom's mechanics on synthetic ranges of a few frames to 3.5 s set the spacing to 0, at which every rule is the one before (`test_renderer_effects.py`, `test_renderer_media.py`, `test_hf_gate.py`, `test_clip_cutaway.py`, one test each of `test_renderer_edit.py`, `test_engine_contract.py` and the base page golden of `test_hf_page.py`); the whole render of `test_renderer_render.py` that compares a zoomed and a flat picture joins the fixture's sentences in pairs, so that some of its ranges outlast the spacing, and checks the first range the spacing zooms. The payload rows golden of `test_renderer_common.py` and the HyperFrames picture cutaway's hashes (`golden/hf-cutaway-picture.sha256`) moved; with the spacing at 0 the payload rows are the snap's golden byte for byte. `test_zoom_spacing.py` covers the spacing.

**The serif: Playfair Display 900, not yet in the repository.** The pick is Playfair Display at weight 900 (SIL Open Font License 1.1): a high-contrast display serif whose heavy weight has narrow, tall capitals, the closest open match to the reference's narrow bold serif among the fonts that are on Fontsource with a Latin static subset (the channel the three bundled fonts came from), and the Latin subset draws Portuguese. Candidates set aside: Bodoni Moda (as contrasted, but wider at its heaviest), Libre Caslon Condensed (narrow, but tops out at 700 and its contrast is lower), Noto Serif Display Condensed (its narrow widths are not in a static subset), Instrument Serif (narrow, but one regular weight only). The file is not committed: downloading it needs the person's own approval, which this package did not have. Adding it is the maintainer's step, and changes no code: put `playfair-display-900.woff2` (Fontsource `@fontsource/playfair-display`, the Latin subset of weight 900) and the project's `OFL.txt` as `PlayfairDisplay-OFL.txt` into `providers/renderer/assets/fonts/`, add a row `{id: playfair-display-900, file: playfair-display-900.woff2, sha256, weight: 900, family: "Playfair Display", licence: OFL-1.1, licence_file: PlayfairDisplay-OFL.txt, source}` to `look_fonts`, and rebuild the image. Until then `headline-serif` draws with the fallback stack (DejaVu Serif Bold in the image).

**The image.** The ffmpeg engine's image tag is the hash of its files (`image-manifest.json`); this package changes `renderer-presets.json`, `edl.schema.json`, `_edl.py`, `_media.py`, `_layout.py`, `_payload.py`, `_assets.py`, `_render.py`, `_preview.py`, `_inner.py`, `_hf_compose.py`, `_hf_page.py`, `_hf_gate.py` and `assets/fonts/allowlist.json`, so the image is stale until the maintainer runs `python3 providers/renderer/ffmpeg.py build --confirmed` (and again after the serif's file is added). The blocks are staged per job and need no rebuild.

**The clip fit of the media step.** `runtime/step_media.fit_clip_entries` shortens a cutaway to fit its clip by modelling the renderer's beat length; since the snap can lengthen a beat (up to 0.25 s earlier, 0.5 s later), it now adds the most the snap can add at that beat's position (the same cuts, the windows copied as `SNAP_END_SECONDS` and `SNAP_START_SECONDS`, compared with `_edl`'s by the constants test), so a fitted clip cutaway still never holds a frame on ffmpeg nor meets `clip_too_short` on HyperFrames (its seeded sweep found the case).

**Not changed.** The vocabulary and `mkt-social-copy`; the pill's page path; the slow push-in; `left` and `right` splits; the HyperFrames blocks (its goldens moved by the snap's timing only); the shape of the compose graph (`_media.py` changes only which ranges are zoomed).

**Verified.** The renderer's tests and the tests `scripts/select_tests.py` picks, on the host (Python, the host's headless browser for the pages, the host's ffmpeg for the compose), `scripts/validate.py` and `scripts/security_scan.py`; and a host render (no container) of the real reel with each headline look, checked on stills (the aside ends on the cut, the seam chips and the headlines sit clear of the face). **Not verified.** A render in the container (the image was not rebuilt), the Playfair face itself (only a stand-in file proved the look-font wiring, and the fallback stack was drawn), the caption's entry animation playing again where a headline or a split cuts a chunk, and the look of the seam chip and the headline on more than one real recording.
