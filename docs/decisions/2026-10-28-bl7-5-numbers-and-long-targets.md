## 2026-10-28: a message number the model gives opens the family, and a long sender-and-subject target is grounded (BL7-5, limits L54, L61 and L63, no model call, no lab run)

The first blind sets showed two mail lines that the product refused although the model had understood them (SPEC-BL7-4 section 9, gaps G1 and G3). Package BL7-5 builds both; the prompt, the catalog, the protocol and the enabling entry are unchanged, and no rule of L54, L61 or L63 changes (L61 gains two sentences in its description).

- **G1, numbers.** `ops_interpret._carry_out` tries the closed forms first, as before. When they read nothing, the operation is a mail operation that names messages (`MAIL_TARGET_OPS`), the project's language is pt-BR or en and the model gave `message` or `messages`, `ops_natural_mail.mail_number_seed` opens the family with a seed `{"op", "by": "numbers", "slots": {"numbers": [...]}}` (a reply or a reply draft adds its body, a move its folder, a mark its state when the span reads one). A seed exists only when the decision's spans hold none of `target`, `day`, `to` or `subject`, and the numbers the model gave are EXACTLY the line's free numbers (`natural_writes_mail.free_numbers`: whole numbers that are words of their own, outside the texts of the decision, outside every time phrase of `natural_time.find`, not followed by a unit or a currency mark, not preceded by a currency; a range such as `1-3` or `1 a 3` gives none; an unmappable time hit gives none). The numbers become `<source>#<n>` of the newest snapshot (origin `line-number`), a numbered line never refreshes a snapshot, a number past the end, a snapshot older than 26 hours and a caller without `ask-mail` are refused by `item_refs` when the operation runs, and a reply still goes only to the index's From (L60). `ops_natural_writes._read` accepts `by` `numbers` beside `target`. S41-11's inventory must list `mail_number_seed` as what replaces the numbered mail heads when it deletes the closed ones (L63 (3)).
- **G3, the ground.** `interpreter._own_subject_split` grounds a mail `target` that the closed rule reads as no words (more than six content words) and that holds a subject marker after the sender (`SUBJECT_MARKERS`, from the second word on, with a word after it): the ground is the sender and the subject without the marker word (`_subject_ground`), at most `natural_writes_mail.GROUND_WORDS_MAX` (10) content words. It is recorded as `target_ground`, and the sender's part as `target_sender` (nothing reads it yet: OPEN-BL7-5-E, so that GROUNDING-FROM or a later package can require the sender's words in the From). `mail_target_seed` reads a ground with `ground_words`; `match_words` is untouched and still needs EVERY word, so the cap only decides between a search and a refusal and the ground never reaches a message with fewer of the target's words. `target_words` gained a keyword `cap` and its output with no `cap` is byte-identical (20 recorded targets in `test_mail_long_target.py`).
