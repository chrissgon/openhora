## 2026-10-30: the full fuzz of the private-line words ran a third slower and filled the memory: the frozen oracle copied the language tables on every call, and the deadline reading was quadratic (fuzz-slow-deadlines; no model call, no lab run, fingerprint unchanged)

**What was seen.** In the whole-suite CI run of PR #668 (`full-suite` label, so `WB_FULL_FUZZ=1`) `test_private_words_flag_a_superset_of_surface_mutated_lines[201]` took 88 s, `..._of_generated_and_mutated_lines[102]` went past the 120 s `faulthandler_timeout` and its worker crashed (`node down: Not properly terminated`; the single-process rerun of the failed test ended with exit code 139, a segmentation fault, 11 s after the timeout dump). The same tests had taken 6 to 14 s in the run of PR #649.

**Not a regression: the size.** The run of #649 was a selected-mode run, so `WB_FULL_FUZZ` was unset and the tests read 3,000 and 8,000 lines; `WB_FULL_FUZZ=1` makes them read 60,000 and 160,000 (twenty times as many). The step from 6 to 14 s to 88 s and 212 s is that size, not a change of code. Locally, with `WB_FULL_FUZZ=1`, at `34102d10` (#649): generated [102] 70.1 s, surface [201] 19.8 s.

**A regression of a third, found by timing each commit.** Same machine, same flag, the two tests run alone (`generated [102]` / `surface [201]`): `34102d10` 70.1 / 19.8 s; `64c9262a` (PRIVATE-CALENDAR-HEAD, #659) 70.6 / 19.9 s; `bbfb29ad` (CANDIDATE-OUT 2, #658) 97.0 / 25.4 s; `ecaba7d1` 97.4 / 25.4 s; the tip `cf53cea5` 94.8 to 97.7 / 25.6 to 25.8 s (a factor of 1.37 and 1.28). CANDIDATE-OUT 2 moved the old `natural.candidate` into the test tree as `runtime/tests/old_claim.py`, the oracle of the fuzz (`closed_heads.old_private`). Its helper `_frozen_tables` made a NEW copy of the language tables on every call. `natural_time._comp` keeps the compiled patterns of a table by the table's identity (`_compiled[id(table)]`) and dropped none, so every line the oracle was asked about built the patterns again (a profile of 6,000 lines: 4,344 `_Compiled.__init__` calls where the commit before had none) and kept them for good. Measured on the generated lines of seed 102: 233 MB resident after 5,000 lines, 806 MB after 20,000, 62,172 entries in `_compiled`, about 40 kB a line; the full fuzz asks the oracle about 160,000 lines. That growth is the likely cause of the crash of the CI worker (a slower and a smaller machine than this one; not reproduced there).

**The fix.**
1. `old_claim._frozen_tables` makes its copy of a live table once and keeps it (by the table's identity, the way `_comp` does). Behaviour identical: the same tables, the same keys. Resident memory stays at 59 MB over 20,000 lines; 20,000 lines in 8.4 s instead of 11.7 s.
2. `natural_time._comp` keeps at most `COMPILED_MAX` (64) tables and drops the cache when it would pass it: a caller that makes a new table object for every call can no longer grow it without bound. Behaviour identical (a dropped entry is built again when asked for).
3. `natural_time._deadlines` read the text before EACH hit (`re.findall` over `norm[:s]`, three times) and the list of ALL hits for each hit, so its time grew with the square of the line. This is the function the timeout dump of the crash showed, and it reads text a person sends from a channel. It now builds its tables once (the tokens with a bisect, the hits by their end with a running "a moment is before", the last clock) and reads nothing for a hit that is not a date or a slash date. The split it returns is identical: `runtime/tests/test_time_deadline_linear.py` checks it against the old function, kept there as the oracle, on 60,000 random lines with random hit lists (every kind, overlapping, cut inside a word, the deadline, destination and break words of both languages): no difference; and with one condition left out of the oracle the cases do differ, so they reach it. On 50,000 characters of a moment, a clock and then "due on 15th" (4,167 hits), `_deadlines` took 4.14 s before and 0.02 s now; of "at 9am" again and again (7,142 hits), 7.76 s before and 0.003 s now (the test fails on the old code with exactly those numbers). The rest of `find` is still quadratic in the line (`_keep`, `_neighbours`, `_extend`, the overlap check of `_scan`): `find` on 10,000 characters of clocks took 1.39 s before and takes 0.67 s now, on 40,000 20.9 s and 10.9 s. A message cap or a rewrite of those readers is a decision of its own (the channels cap a message near 4,000 characters, where `find` takes about 0.1 s); this entry does not take it.

**Measured after the fix** (`WB_FULL_FUZZ=1`, the two tests alone, the tip run at the same time on the same machine): tip 94.8 / 25.8 s, fixed 70.4 / 20.1 s, the numbers of `34102d10`. The golden of the entry replay, `entry_ext/` and the two phrase snapshots are unchanged; `test_entry_replay.py` passes. What is NOT changed: the two fuzz tests still take 70 s of one core locally with the full flag, so a CI machine that is twice as slow would still pass 120 s with the first of them; whether to split them into shards the way the other full-fuzz tests are (one line in four of the same lines, `runtime/README.md`, "The entry replay") is left to the next entry if the CI run of this pull request shows it.
