## 2026-10-28: the search's pre-state and one listen test no longer depend on a timer being faster than the machine (FLAKY-RESEARCH-PRESTATE-2, test-only, no model call, no provider call, no lab run, fingerprint unchanged)

Two flakes of the test kit, each with its cause found and proved deterministically, no retry and no longer sleep.

- **The search's pre-state (`entry_kit.prestate_research_running`).** Cause, from the state the pre-state was in when it failed (about 6 of 100 runs on a machine under heavy load: 53 of 900 in the measurement, the three scenarios of the search alike): the pre-state set the acknowledgement delay to 0.2 s before the owner asked. When the line that asks the question took longer than that, its acknowledgement timer fired and the send began; the job then ended and sent the question (the timer is stopped before the question, but a send that has begun is not undone), and the acknowledgement reached the chat after it. The question was no longer the last message, so the owner's yes was answered "to confirm, reply yes to the question..." and no search began ("the search began after the owner's yes" never held). The same load let the held search reach the kit's 10 s bound and answer before the line, so the outcome lacked the search's answer. Change: the line that asks the question gets no acknowledgement (the delay is 3600 s while it runs and 0.2 s only from the owner's yes, whose acknowledgement is waited for as "a message after the question, and the chat's last message is set"), and the held search waits `HELD_SECONDS` (120 s, a bound against a hung test) instead of `WAIT`: the line releases it. Proof: `test_an_acknowledgement_that_is_slow_to_send_does_not_change_the_search_prestate` makes the call that answers a line take 0.3 s and an acknowledgement take 0.5 s to send, which is that order on any machine: the three scenarios of the search differ from the golden before the change and equal it after. Repeated, with no load generator and inside one test slot: the three search scenarios replayed 40 times each (120 of 120 equal to the golden), the listen crash test and its ledger-delay variant 30 times each (60 of 60), the slow-acknowledgement proof 30 times for each scenario (90 of 90); and all four failures that TEST-SPEED's CI named come from this one pre-state.
- **`test_messaging_listen::test_a_crash_restarts_the_provider_and_the_card_is_sent_again_and_the_first_copys_button_is_stale`.** Cause: the test waits for the provider's log to hold two sends and then taps the second copy's button, but the provider logs a send before the bridge has written its ledger record of the card (the send returns to the bridge first); a tap on a card with no record is `STALE`, so `approve` was never called (`assert ([])` on CI). Change: wait until the bridge's ledger holds the second copy (`ledger.by_action`). Proof: `test_the_crash_test_does_not_race_the_bridges_ledger_write` writes every record 0.4 s late: the old test fails on it with the CI's own message, the new one passes. No production code changed.
